The New Dog Essentials Checklist: Exactly What to Buy Before Day One

By The First 90 Days Team · Published September 11, 2026 · 9 min read

Walk into a pet store as a first-time owner and you'll leave $400 lighter with things you don't need. Here's the exact list — 12 must-haves, 6 nice-to-haves, 5 things to skip — with what to look for in each, so you buy for week one, not year one.

The philosophy: buy for week 1, not year 1

The most common gear mistake new owners make is buying the finished setup on day one: the orthopedic bed, the full wardrobe, the bulk food subscription. Then the puppy chews the bed, outgrows the harness in six weeks, and hates the food. Week-one gear should be functional, correctly sized for now, and cheap enough to replace without grief. Buy the nice versions in month 3 or 4, once you know your dog.

Buy before day one: the 12 must-haves

  1. Crate with divider panel. Size for the dog's adult weight; use the divider to shrink it for the puppy. Wire crates with dividers included are the best value. See our breed-by-breed sizing chart before you order.
  2. 6-foot leash (not retractable). Flat nylon or leather, with a sturdy clasp. Retractable leashes teach pulling and are a burn/injury risk — skip them permanently, not just for now.
  3. Collar + ID tag. Flat buckle collar you can fit two fingers under. Get the tag engraved before day one: your phone number and "REWARD" if you like. A dog with no tag is a dog that stays lost.
  4. Harness (front-clip). For puppies and pullers, a front-clip harness manages pulling while you train loose-leash walking. Measure the chest; size up if between sizes for a growing puppy.
  5. Stainless steel food and water bowls. Heavy enough not to slide, dishwasher-safe, and they don't harbor bacteria like scratched plastic. Skip the cute ceramic until the chewing phase passes.
  6. Two weeks of their current food. Whatever the breeder or shelter is feeding — buy the same thing. Food switches cause diarrhea; you'll transition gradually later. Ask for the exact brand and formula before pickup day.
  7. Training treats. Small (pea-sized), soft, and smelly — you'll burn through hundreds in month one. Buy a big bag; running out mid-training-week is a real problem. Cut larger treats in half to stretch them.
  8. 2–3 chew toys, different textures. One rubber (stuffable), one rope, one harder nylon or rubber bone. Rotate them — novelty keeps a puppy interested longer than quantity.
  9. One puzzle feeder or slow feeder. A frozen stuffed Kong buys you 20 minutes of peace and teaches the puppy to settle independently. That 20 minutes is worth more than any other item on this list some evenings.
  10. Enzyme cleaner. Not regular cleaner, not vinegar — an enzymatic pet stain remover. Regular cleaners leave the scent proteins behind, and the dog will return to the same spot. This is the #1 forgotten item.
  11. Poop bags — the big box. You'll use 3–5 a day. Buy the 500-count box now; the per-bag cost drops by two-thirds and you won't run out during a rainy week.
  12. A simple dog bed or mat. Not the $120 orthopedic one — a $25 washable mat. It will get chewed, peed on, or both. Buy the nice bed at month 4.

Buy in month 1–2: the 6 nice-to-haves

Don't buy these before day one. By month two you'll know your dog's size, chewing intensity, and habits — and you'll buy the right version once instead of the wrong version twice.

  1. Playpen or baby gates. For giving the puppy safe space beyond the crate — essential if you work from home and need the dog contained but not crated for hours.
  2. Grooming kit. Nail clippers or a grinder, a brush matched to the coat type, and dog shampoo. Learn nail trims early; paying $20 a trim adds up fast.
  3. Car restraint. A crash-tested harness or a back-seat barrier. An unrestrained 40-pound dog in a 30-mph stop becomes a projectile — this is safety gear, not an accessory.
  4. Long line (15–30 ft). For recall training in parks and fields. A biothane long line doesn't tangle or absorb water like nylon.
  5. A second bed. Once the chewing phase eases (usually month 3–4), buy the good one for the living room and demote the cheap mat to the crate.
  6. Treat pouch. Clip-on, with a drawstring top. Fishing treats out of your pocket 40 times a day gets old by day three.

Skip for now: 5 things new owners waste money on

  1. The orthopedic / premium bed. It will be destroyed. Buy it at month 4 as a reward for surviving adolescence.
  2. Retractable leashes. They reward pulling, jam at the worst moments, and cause leash burns. A 6-foot flat leash does everything better.
  3. The wardrobe. Unless you have a thin-coated breed in a cold climate (greyhound in Minnesota, say), your dog does not need outfits. One functional coat for genuine cold is plenty.
  4. Bulk food subscriptions. You don't know what agrees with their stomach yet, or how fast a growing puppy will go through it. Buy small bags until month 3.
  5. Premium everything "just in case." The $60 water bowl, the designer collar, the subscription treat box — month-one money is better spent on the emergency fund. Upgrade later from a position of knowledge.

FAQ

What's the one thing people forget?
The enzyme cleaner, by a mile. Everyone buys the cute stuff — collar, toys, bed — and nobody buys the $14 bottle that determines whether potty training takes four weeks or four months. Buy it before day one, then follow our hour-by-hour potty training schedule so the cleaner gets as little work as possible.
Can I buy secondhand?
Yes for hard goods: crates, baby gates, and playpens are perfect secondhand buys — sanitize with a pet-safe disinfectant and save 40–60%. Say no to secondhand chews, bedding, and food (hygiene and safety), and inspect any used crate for bent wires or broken latches.
